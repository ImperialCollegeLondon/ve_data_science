    Abiotic validation plan

    For each VE abiotic variable we find observations or published values, convert them to the VE unit and the VE monthly time step, record how close they are to the VE grid in space and time, for comparing them with the model output for the same cells and months.

    This plan gives an overview of that process for the abiotic and abiotic simple modules at Maliau: the variables to validate and the data they need or still lack (Section 3), the data in hand (Section 4), how the data are processed (Section 5) and the status of each variable (Section 6).

    File What it holds
    data/primary/validation/maliau_variables_summary.xlsx (globus) VE variables with unit and validation priority (output_variables) and model constants (model_constats)
    data/primary/validation/dataset/ (globus) The raw data from SAFE Zenodo: safe_abiotic/ (microclimate loggers, flux tower meteorology and eddy covariance) and safe_soil/ (soil temperature and moisture surveys; primary forest plots only are used)
    data/primary/validation/abiotic/abiotic_literature_values.csv (globus) Published values and ranges, one row per value, with citation, DOI and status
    data/primary/validation/abiotic/abiotic_sources.toml (globus) Where each dataset is, its columns, units, quality limits, coordinates, citation and screening record
    analysis/abiotic/validation/abiotic_validation_data.py (github) Python script that reads, cleans and converts the data, generates abiotic_validation_data.xlsx and writes the tracker in Section 6
    data/derived/validation/abiotic/abiotic_validation_data.xlsx (globus) Processed data: daily and monthly values, the long table validation_long and the variable_registry (with the data needed or lacking per variable)

    3. Variables and data needed

    Variables and units are from the output_variables sheet of maliau_variables_summary.xlsx, with their priority in the classification, validation_priority and spin_up_sensitive columns. Direct means compared with observations of the same quantity, derived through a quantity in the second table, and literature with published values only. VE output is how the model reports the variable each month (read from the model code). The abiotic model builds one 24-hour day from the monthly forcing (a sine curve for temperature, shortwave radiation spread over the daylight hours) and reports the mean of that day; abiotic simple gives one value per month from the forcing and leaf area index, with no daily cycle. Fluxes come only from the abiotic model. Data needed / lacking is what is still missing to complete the validation of the variable; the same text is in the tracker (Section 6) and in DATA_NEEDED in the script.

    condensation, density_air, latent_heat_vapourisation and specific_heat_air need no field data: they follow standard physical formulas and are checked by unit tests.

    Derived variables combine VE outputs, or observations, so that they match each other. They are candidates for data/derived/validation/derived_variables.toml (step 5 of the validation database).

    Derived variable Formula Unit
    vapour_pressure (observed) $e_a = e_s(T)\,RH / 100$, $e_s(T) = 0.6112 \exp(17.62\,T / (T + 243.12))$, the Magnus equation the VE uses (pyrealm calc_vp_sat, default coefficients of Sonntag 1990) kPa
    vapour_pressure_deficit (observed) $VPD = e_s(T) - e_a$ kPa
    latent_heat_flux from evapotranspiration $LE = ET \cdot \lambda$ / seconds per period (as calculate_latent_heat_flux in the VE), with $\lambda = 1.91846 \times 10^6\,(T_K / (T_K - 33.91))^2$ J kg⁻¹ (Henderson-Sellers 1984, as the VE; about 2.44 MJ kg⁻¹ at 25 °C); the evapotranspiration values of the hydrology plan also check LE W m⁻²
    energy balance closure $G = R_n + LE + H$ with the VE signs (see the sign convention below the table: $R_n$ positive when the surface gains energy, $LE$ and $H$ negative from the surface to the air, $G$ positive into the soil); in the VE the topsoil $G$ is this residual W m⁻²
    diurnal_temperature_range (observed) monthly mean of daily maximum − daily minimum air temperature °C
    above-canopy net_radiation, latent_heat_flux, sensible_heat_flux sum of the VE profile over the canopy layers, surface layer and topsoil, compared with the flux tower; the VE stores LE and H as negative from the surface to the air, so the tower LE and H change sign (obs_in_ve_unit) W m⁻²

    Sign convention of the energy balance. The plan uses the VE signs for each term of the energy balance:

    • $R_n$ (net radiation) is positive when the surface gains energy, that is, it absorbs more radiation from the sun and sky than it emits.
    • $LE$ (latent heat flux) and $H$ (sensible heat flux) are negative when heat leaves the surface (soil or leaves) and goes into the air, and positive when heat goes from the air to the surface.
    • $G$ (ground heat flux) is positive when heat goes down into the soil.

    With these signs the balance is $G = R_n + LE + H$: the soil receives the radiation energy the surface gains ($R_n$), less the energy used to evaporate water ($LE$) and warm the air ($H$). The VE computes the topsoil $G$ this way, as the residual. The SAFE flux tower reports $LE$ and $H$ as positive from the surface to the air, so obs_in_ve_unit holds them with the VE sign.

    Inputs and constants the same data can check: the ERA5-Land forcing (air temperature, relative humidity, pressure and wind against the flux tower above the canopy); surface_albedo and leaf_albedo; the soil constants marked as placeholders in the code (soil_porosity, soil_thermal_conductivity_dry, soil_thermal_conductivity_saturated) through soil temperature at 5, 10 and 20 cm; and the leaf area index gradients in AbioticSimpleBounds (from Hardwick et al. 2015) through the loggers. Values are in the model_constats sheet.

    Model inputs that the comparison also needs, beyond the validation data: leaf area index and canopy height for the maliau_2 cells (they set the VE layers and drive the abiotic simple gradients), latitude set to Maliau, and the logger and sensor heights to pick the matching VE layer.

    5. Processing to VE units and monthly values

    The script abiotic_validation_data.py does every step below; each quality-control step is logged in the workbook's qc_log sheet; the settings (gaps, completeness, quality flag) are in the script's settings section. Files are under the dataset folder (data/primary/validation/dataset/) unless the path is given in full.

    Data Files processed Steps to a monthly value Conversion to the VE unit
    Air temperature (loggers) safe_abiotic/Microclimate1319.csv Remove embedded header rows; values outside 10-45 °C set missing; mean per plot and local hour; fill gaps up to 3 hours; daily mean, maximum and minimum from days with at least 20 hours; monthly mean from months with at least 20 complete days °C, no conversion
    Relative humidity (loggers) safe_abiotic/Microclimate1319.csv Values from 100 % to 130 % capped at 100 %; then as air temperature %, no conversion
    Vapour pressure and VPD safe_abiotic/Microclimate1319.csv Calculated hourly from temperature and RH (Section 3), then as air temperature kPa, no conversion
    Flux tower meteorology safe_abiotic/SAFE_FluxTower_MetData_2012-2017 .xlsx (sheets data_above canopy, data_below canopy) Values outside limits set missing (air temperature 10-45 °C, pressure 900-1100 mbar, net radiation, soil temperature 15-40 °C); half-hours to hourly means; gaps up to 3 hours filled; daily and monthly means with the logger rules, per variable; vapour pressure and VPD above the canopy from temperature and RH Net radiation × −1 (the file counts energy to the atmosphere as positive), W m⁻²; pressure mbar / 10 = kPa; soil temperature at 5, 10 and 20 cm, °C
    Eddy covariance safe_abiotic/SAFE_EC_byYear.xlsx (sheets Raw_data_2012_2013 to Raw_data_2018) -9999 set missing and repeated time stamps dropped; LE and H kept when their quality flag is at most 3 (flux_qc_max, to agree) and within −200 to 1000 W m⁻²; LE from 2012-12 to 2013-02 dropped (midday LE near 0 W m⁻² while H is normal: the water vapour signal failed); monthly value = mean of the month's mean diurnal course, only when every hour of the day has at least 2 valid half-hours LE, H W m⁻² and wind speed m s⁻¹, no conversion
    Soil temperature and soil moisture (primary forest plots) safe_soil/SAFE_SoilRespiration_Data_SAFEdatabase_update_2021-01-11.xlsx, safe_soil/Carbon_corrected_slopes.xlsx, safe_soil/Termite_monitoring_maliau.xlsx Keep primary forest rows ([sources.keep]); soil temperature outside 10-45 °C and soil moisture outside 0-100 % set missing; monthly value = mean of all readings per plot and month (visit_mean) Soil temperature °C, no conversion; soil moisture % / 100 = θ (m³ m⁻³), VE value θ × 250 mm
    Literature data/primary/validation/abiotic/abiotic_literature_values.csv Only extracted rows; a range becomes two rows Annual values against the mean of monthly VE values; latent heat flux from evapotranspiration as in Section 3

    Checks of the flux tower values (2026-10-13): VPD calculated from the 50 m temperature and RH matches the eddy covariance VPD (median 0.36 kPa for both); LE matches the ET column × 2.45 MJ kg⁻¹; the energy balance closure (LE + H) / Rn with the tower signs, or −(LE + H) / Rn with the VE signs, is 0.55-0.82 per month, usual for tropical forest towers; the net radiation in the file is negative at midday, which confirms the sign reversal. The eddy covariance air pressure is about 100.7 kPa against 97.4 kPa from the barometer, so pressure comes from the barometer only.

    Every row is then classed by its distance to the grid and its period:

    Match Classes
    Spatial inside the maliau_2 grid (match); within 100 km of it (partial); further away (no match)
    Temporal in run (match); in run but needs daily output, such as mean daily maxima, or partly in run (partial); outside run (no match)

    The result is the validation_long sheet in data/derived/validation/abiotic/abiotic_validation_data.xlsx: one row per observation in the columns of the validation database (location, time window, original and converted value), plus the value in VE terms (ve_variable, ve_unit, obs_in_ve_unit, the observed value in the VE unit, not model output), where to read the VE value (ve_compare_at: the grid mean for every abiotic variable) and the match classes.

    7. Next steps

    1. Choose the VE layer to compare with the loggers (1 m above ground).
    2. Extract the to_extract literature values.
    3. Agree the eddy covariance quality flag threshold (flux_qc_max, now 3). Few months pass the diurnal-course rule: 9 for latent heat flux, 23 for sensible heat flux and 26 for wind speed.
    4. Collect or derive the data in the data needed / lacking column. Three gaps can be closed with data already in hand: diurnal_temperature_range (logger and tower daily maxima and minima), atmospheric_co2 (co2_mole_fraction) and aerodynamic_resistance_canopy above the canopy (wind speed and friction velocity in SAFE_EC_byYear.xlsx).
    5. Check the termite control plots' soil moisture of about 2 % in the 2016-03 drought.

    Points to agree on before comparing:

    • Where on the grid: every cell gets the same ERA5-Land forcing, so differences between cells come only from the land surface. Proposed: the grid mean against the mean of the Maliau old-growth loggers as the main target, the cells holding the OG2 plots as a check, and the spread across cells against the spread across plots as a test of spatial variation.
    • Which layer: the loggers are 1-1.5 m above the ground, and the VE has no layer at 1 m: the surface layer is at 0.1 m and the lowest canopy layer depends on the canopy. Proposed: the surface layer, with the lowest canopy layer as a check; not the reference height 2 m above the canopy.
    • Which model: compare abiotic and abiotic simple in the same way?
    • The logger period (2013-2019) and the flux tower period (2012-2018) are inside the run (2010-2020); the SAFE plots and the flux tower are regional checks only.
    • Monthly means of one modelled day: the VE value is the mean of one 24-hour day built from the monthly forcing, so compare it with the mean of the month's mean diurnal course (as the script does for the flux tower), not with single days.
    • Flux tower height: the tower measures 25-27 m above the canopy, higher than the VE reference height (2 m above the canopy), so above-canopy air temperature, humidity and wind are a range check only.

    Possible model issues found while reading the code (inferred from the code, not yet tested; to raise with the VE developers before reading the results):

    • Day length: the daily cycle uses 12 + 4 cos((month − 1) π / 6) cos(latitude) hours, which at Maliau gives about 16 hours in January and 8 hours in July instead of about 12 hours all year.
    • Shortwave radiation in the modelled day: fixed in the current model code (the hourly weights are now multiplied by 24, so the day's mean equals the monthly mean).
    • Diurnal temperature range: the forcing range is used as the amplitude of the temperature sine curve, so the modelled day spans twice that range.
    • abiotic simple net radiation has no incoming longwave term, and its longwave emission is calculated from the temperature in °C, not kelvin (calculate_longwave_emission expects kelvin; the abiotic model adds 273.15), so the emission is close to 0 W m⁻² and net radiation is close to the absorbed shortwave.
